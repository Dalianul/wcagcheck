European Accessibility Act · Article 13 and Annex V

The European Accessibility Act does not require an accessibility statement

Almost every guide, and every free generator we could find, tells a private company to publish an “accessibility statement” with a compliance status and a list of what is broken. The Act never uses that phrase, and it asks for something different. Here is the actual text.

What the Act says

The obligation on a service provider is in Article 13(2) of Directive (EU) 2019/882:

Service providers shall prepare the necessary information in accordance with Annex V and shall explain how the services meet the applicable accessibility requirements. The information shall be made available to the public in written and oral format, including in a manner which is accessible to persons with disabilities. Service providers shall keep that information for as long as the service is in operation.

Note what it asks for: information, kept public for as long as the service runs, in writing and in speech. Not a statement, not a status, not a confession.

The three things Annex V asks for

Annex V puts the information in your general terms and conditions, or an equivalent document, and lists the elements it must contain where applicable:

  1. a general description of the service in accessible formats;
  2. descriptions and explanations necessary for understanding how the service operates;
  3. a description of how the relevant accessibility requirements in Annex I are met.

There is a further point: you must be able to show that the way you deliver and monitor the service keeps it compliant. That is an operational duty, not a paragraph.

Only the third element is about conformance, and it is the only one a scanner can help you with. The first two describe your own service, and nobody can write them for you.

Why you keep being handed the wrong template

The document everyone means by “accessibility statement” is real, official, and belongs to a different directive. The Web Accessibility Directive, Directive (EU) 2016/2102, applies to public sector bodies. Its Article 7 requires a statement, and Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2018/1523 lays down the model, whose mandatory sections are:

  • a compliance status — fully compliant, partially compliant, or not compliant;
  • the non-accessible content, with the reason given as non-compliance, disproportionate burden, or out of scope;
  • when the statement was prepared, by what method, and when it was last reviewed;
  • a feedback mechanism, with a link;
  • a link to the enforcement procedure.

Every one of those is absent from the EAA. The structures differ because the purposes differ: the public sector statement exists to disclose and justify inaccessibility that the law tolerates under narrow exemptions. The EAA does not tolerate it the same way, so it does not ask you to catalogue it. Non-compliance is dealt with through market surveillance and corrective action.

Germany’s federal accessibility body, the Bundesfachstelle Barrierefreiheit, draws the line explicitly: the BFSG information is to be distinguished from the accessibility declaration under the BGG, because under the BFSG the provider is fundamentally obliged to achieve accessibility, so non-conformity does not have to be stated and justified.

The Decision cannot have been written for the EAA in any case. It was adopted in October 2018 and the EAA in April 2019, and its own Article 1 scopes it to public sector bodies under Directive (EU) 2016/2102.

If you are in Germany, there is a fourth element

The BFSG transposes the EAA, and Anlage 3 repeats Annex V’s three elements and adds one of its own: you must name the competent market surveillance authority (die Angabe der zuständigen Marktüberwachungsbehörde). This is a genuine national addition above the EU floor, and a template written against Annex V alone will be missing it.

If you are in Romania

Legea nr. 232/2022 transposes the EAA and Anexa nr. 5 is a direct translation of Annex V — the same three elements, nothing added. Supervision is split by sector under Article 19: ANPC, ANCOM, ADR, CNA, the transport ministry and the competition council each take different service categories, so which authority is yours depends on what you sell.

“Compliant”, not “conformant”

The binding EU text uses compliant. Decision 2018/1523 says fully compliant, partially compliant, not compliant, and never uses “conformant” for that status. The conformance vocabulary comes from W3C, where it describes meeting a technical standard rather than a legal instrument. The two are used interchangeably almost everywhere, but if you are modelling your text on the Commission’s, use its word.
